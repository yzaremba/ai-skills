Review the current conversation and share a self-reflection — but only if you have something genuinely critical or thoughtful to say.

**High bar.** No nitpicks, no mild advice, no filler. If the reflection would be "nice to have" rather than "meaningfully changes how we work," leave it out. An empty response ("Nothing critical to flag.") is a valid and preferred outcome over padded observations.

When you do have something worth saying, use whichever of these sections fit — omit any that would be empty. Do not force all three.

- **Lessons learned** — concrete moments that mattered: a wrong assumption that cost real effort, a pattern that clearly paid off, a decision point that should have gone the other way. Cite specifics.
- **Worth remembering** — candidates for memory, grouped by type (user / feedback / project / reference). For each, say *why* it's worth saving and *when* it would apply. Only propose items that would actually change future behavior. Do NOT save anything — just propose.
- **Recommendations for the user** — workflow, tooling, or briefing changes that would have made *this* session materially better. Not polish — substance.
