## Writing briefs, reading reports

A role you dispatch starts fresh: it knows only your brief, and you get back only its report.

**A brief states** the goal, in one sentence; the spec, by file and by requirement id or PLAN step id, pointed at and not paraphrased (with no document, as for a hotfix, the brief's own text is the spec and says so); the working directory, in full as the session shows it, and the branch; what the role may change and what it must leave alone; what is decided and ruled out, with the user's decisions quoted; whether the user will talk to the role in its thread or there is no dialogue; how the work is verified; whether to commit, and the message, where no ask means no commit; and anything the report must hold beyond its standard parts.

**A report is a claim.** Check what is cheap to check, such as that a commit exists, then relay the conclusion in a few lines and point at the files.
